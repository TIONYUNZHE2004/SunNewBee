# TownshipLens — Original Idea (v0, as submitted by the team)

> Verbatim snapshot of the team's original concept note (Oct 2026). The living version of the brief is [project-brief.md](project-brief.md). Do not edit this file — record changes as decisions in [decisions/](decisions/).

### **An AI assistant that understands your township—not just its documents.**

## 1. Summary

**TownshipLens is a resident-facing AI assistant grounded in a captured 3D model of a real township environment.**

Residents ask everyday questions in natural language. The assistant uses verified spatial information, facility details and operational updates to answer—and highlights the relevant locations or routes in an interactive 3D webpage.

Behind the scenes, task-based resident agents test different needs and journeys to uncover information gaps and access barriers.

**The goal is not to simulate an entire society. It is to help real residents understand and navigate their community, while helping township teams improve the information they maintain.**

---

## 2. The problem

Township information is often fragmented across maps, notices, websites and staff knowledge.

A resident may know where a facility is but still not know:
- Which entrance to use.
- Whether the route contains stairs.
- Where seating, toilets or shelter are located.
- Whether a walkway is closed.
- Whether accessibility information is verified.

**A location pin or FAQ does not always answer a question about experiencing a physical place.**

TownshipLens investigates and addresses this gap. Its initial focus is **inclusive access and everyday facility discovery**.

---

## 3. The solution

TownshipLens connects four components:

| Component | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **3D capture** | Provides a recognisable visual representation of the environment |
| **Spatial knowledge model** | Represents places, entrances, paths, connections and verified attributes |
| **Grounded AI assistant** | Answers questions using retrieval and spatial tools |
| **Task-based resident agents** | Test journeys and identify missing information or barriers |

The AI does not treat the 3D scene as a complete source of truth. Critical facts are structured, verified and timestamped.

### Example interaction

**Resident asks:**
> "Can I take a stroller from this parking area to the community hall without using stairs?"

**TownshipLens:**
1. Identifies the parking area and hall entrances.
2. Checks verified path connections and barriers.
3. Highlights a suitable route, if one is confirmed.
4. Explains the route using relevant images and annotations.
5. Clearly identifies any unverified section.

If the information is insufficient, it says so rather than inventing an answer.

---

## 4. Core features

### A. Interactive 3D township explorer
Residents can explore a captured environment, select facilities and inspect annotated entrances or paths.

A 2D alternative supports devices or users for whom 3D is unsuitable.

### B. Conversational spatial assistant
Users ask questions such as:
- "Which entrance should I use with a stroller?"
- "Where are the nearest toilets and rest points?"
- "Which community space has seating and shelter nearby?"
- "How do I reach the hall without stairs?"

Answers link to specific places and supporting evidence.

### C. Verified spatial world model
The model combines:
- Physical geometry and path connectivity.
- Facility names and services.
- Accessibility attributes.
- Opening hours and published closures.
- Evidence sources and verification dates.

For this project, **"world model" means a structured representation of the environment**, not a general-purpose AI model capable of predicting everything.

### D. Resident-task simulation
Agents representing different task constraints attempt journeys through the model.

Examples include:
- A stroller user requiring a step-free route.
- An older resident needing regular rest points.
- A wheelchair user requiring verified accessible connections.

A structured engine checks feasibility. AI helps generate test cases and explain failures. These agents are **testing tools, not substitutes for real resident consultation**.

### E. Knowledge-gap resolution
When a question exposes missing information:
1. The assistant flags the uncertainty.
2. A verification request is recorded.
3. Authorised staff inspect or correct the information.
4. The world model is updated.
5. Future answers use the verified knowledge.

**This feedback loop is the project's main differentiator.**

## 5. Users and value

| User | Intended benefit |
|---|---|
| **Residents** | Easier discovery of facilities and clearer journey information |
| **Visitors** | Better understanding of entrances and unfamiliar spaces |
| **Residents with access needs** | More explicit information about physical barriers and unknowns |
| **Township operations teams** | Visibility into recurring questions, missing data and correction requests |
| **Facility managers** | A practical way to keep local information current |

These benefits will be tested during the prototype and pilot rather than presented as established outcomes.

---

## 6. Prototype scope

The hackathon MVP will cover **one community facility and its surrounding public-access environment**, subject to permission.

### Included
- One captured 3D scene.
- Several entrances and connecting paths.
- Annotated toilets, seating and shelter.
- A verified spatial graph.
- A conversational interface.
- Route and facility highlighting.
- Evidence and uncertainty labels.
- A staff correction interface.
- Three task-based resident profiles.

### Not included initially
- Full-township reconstruction.
- Continuous resident tracking.
- Live crowd or flood detection.
- Safety guarantees.
- Predictions of residents' opinions or future behaviour.
- Automated accessibility-compliance certification.

## 7. Technical approach

```text
Permitted 3D capture + field verification + facility information
                              ↓
                Spatial graph and knowledge store
                              ↓
Resident question → AI selects approved tools
                              ↓
       Routing / facility queries / information retrieval
                              ↓
    Answer + highlighted location + evidence + uncertainty
                              ↓
             Verification request when necessary
                              ↓
                   Authorised model update
```

**Suggested stack:** React, Three.js/React Three Fiber, Python/FastAPI, NetworkX and a structured database, with an LLM supporting tool use and grounded explanations.

The visual scene may use photogrammetry, LiDAR capture or permitted existing models. Critical dimensions and accessibility attributes require separate verification.

---

## 8. Validation and success measures

Compare TownshipLens against a simple map-and-FAQ baseline using real user tasks.

Measure:
- Correctness of answers and routes.
- Frequency of unsupported claims.
- Time needed to find the appropriate entrance or facility.
- User understanding of uncertainty.
- Staff effort required to verify and update information.
- Information gaps discovered through task testing.

Use consent-based research and avoid unnecessary personal data. Images should exclude or obscure identifiable people and sensitive details.

---

## 9. Hackathon fit

**Primary track: People**

TownshipLens supports:
- Accessibility and inclusion.
- Getting around.
- Digital living.
- Community wellbeing.

Its pilot pathway is straightforward: deploy at one permitted facility, test with residents and staff, verify the results, then assess expansion to additional locations.

### Mechanism
Make township knowledge spatially specific, queryable and maintainable.

### Enabler
Combine 3D capture, structured spatial data, AI tool use and task-based agents.

---

## 10. Pitch statement

> **TownshipLens turns a captured township environment into a resident-facing AI assistant. It helps people find the right entrance, understand their route and discover nearby facilities—with evidence rather than guesswork. When information is missing, it helps township teams close the gap, making the community easier to understand and use over time.**
